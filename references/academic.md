# Academic mode / 学术写作

Use for manuscripts, abstracts, figure legends, response letters, scientific cover letters, and research proposals.

- Follow the logic: question → approach → direct result → justified interpretation. Not every paragraph needs all four.
- Separate measured results, model predictions, hypotheses, and speculation.
- Preserve numerical results, units, experimental conditions, citations, and qualifiers exactly unless correction is requested.
- Prefer concrete verbs: measured, observed, identified, compared, reduced, increased, supported.
- Avoid unearned novelty claims: groundbreaking, unprecedented, paradigm-shifting, revolutionary, transformative.
- Replace generic “opens new avenues” claims with a specific capability or limitation.
- Do not substitute “demonstrate” for “suggest” when the evidence is indirect.
- Do not turn association into causation or docking predictions into validated binding.
- Keep the journal's expected formality. Natural academic prose is not casual prose.
- For cover letters, make significance specific and tied to evidence, not hype.

Revision check: Can a reviewer identify what was actually done, what was measured, and what remains unproven?
